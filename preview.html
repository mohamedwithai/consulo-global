Specialist Markets — adding Electrification & Energy Technologies

Original client feedback

“I’d like to add a seventh specialist market: ELECTRIFICATION & ENERGY TECHNOLOGIES. […] I’ve deliberately changed the existing ‘Combustion, Thermal & Energy’ category to ‘Combustion & Thermal Technologies’ so we’re not using ‘Energy’ across two separate categories.”

“It could also be interesting visually to communicate the relationship between Automation & Motion Control ↔ Electrification & Energy Technologies ↔ Mechanical Power Transmission rather than treating every market as existing completely independently.”

Correction made

lib/data.js is the single source of truth for the header mega‑menu, footer, homepage grid, /markets and the dynamic /markets/[id] routes, so the seventh market was added once and propagated to all of them. Markets are now in your stated order, putting the three drive‑related markets consecutively.

Both renames are in. On the combustion entry I also replaced “Energy Systems” in its technology list with “Thermal Efficiency” and “Waste Heat Recovery”, so the category doesn’t reintroduce the “Energy” overlap through its content.

The crossover visual reads horizontally on desktop and stacked on mobile, with the shared technologies printed on the connectors — Variable Speed Drives, Servo Motors, Power Electronics between Automation and Electrification; Electric Motors, Electric Drivetrains, High‑Efficiency Motors between Electrification and Power Transmission. Those are drawn from the three markets’ own technology lists, so the diagram makes an argument rather than drawing arrows.

SECTION 02 cf5cb83

Client Experience / Logos

Original client feedback

“Rather than simply displaying company names, I’d like us to use the actual company logos, subject to the appropriate permissions, and present this section as SELECTED CLIENT EXPERIENCE — Companies we have recruited for across industrial technology.”

“Please also add Pilz and Zimmer Group to the list […] Rather than displaying these randomly, I’d like them grouped by their relevant technology ecosystems. There will be some crossover, which is absolutely fine.”

“I’d also like a smaller selection of the strongest/most recognisable logos displayed on the homepage as an immediate credibility piece, linking through to the fuller Client Experience section.”

Correction made

The old implementation was a flat array of ten strings rendered as text tiles, which couldn’t support grouping (companies need to appear more than once) or real logos. It was replaced with a company list carrying a slug, logo path and featured flag, plus your four ecosystem groups.

On real logos: I can’t embed third‑party trademarks without the permissions you mention, so the section is built to expect them and degrade honestly until they arrive. Each company renders supplied artwork when a logo path is set, and a designed wordmark until then — both states occupy an identical box, so you can switch companies on one at a time as permission comes in with no half‑finished transitional state. Logos render desaturated and lift to colour on hover, keeping twelve brand palettes from fighting the charcoal/yellow scheme.

The homepage strip sits directly beneath the hero rather than further down — as a credibility piece it needs to land before the visitor wonders whether you’ve done this before.

SECTION 03 95168b2

Individual Specialist Market Pages

Original client feedback

“I like the direction of the individual market pages, but I think these need slightly more depth and visual interest. One idea I particularly like is adding a visual ‘How We Map This Market’ section to each page.”

“Direct Technology → Adjacent Technology → Target Organisations → Target Talent […] We can create equivalent maps for all seven specialist markets. This would visually demonstrate that Consulo understands how talent moves between technologies, competitors and adjacent markets rather than simply searching by job title.”

Correction made

Your Automation example is used verbatim. I then wrote equivalent maps for the other six, and the discipline throughout was that the Adjacent Technology row has to be defensible — it is the row carrying your whole argument.

So for Mechanical Power Transmission, adjacency is electric motors, VSDs, belting, linear motion and brakes/clutches: things specified on the same machine by the same buyer. For Distribution & MRO the direct row is product categories and the adjacent row is service and reliability, because that is where the commercial conversation differs. For Combustion & Thermal, adjacency runs into furnaces, waste heat recovery and emissions rather than process instrumentation, which is a different sale.

Each tier carries a one‑line note — “where transferable experience genuinely exists”, “the businesses that employ the people we’re looking for”. Without them the section reads as four more keyword lists; with them it reads as method. The section is headed “We don’t search by job title”, stating the argument the diagram makes.

About / Company Positioning

Original client feedback

“I don’t want any individual Director or Founder photography on the website. I want Consulo Global to have a strong corporate identity in its own right […] The objective is to build Consulo Global as an international specialist search brand rather than a personality‑led recruitment consultancy.”

Correction made

The old page gave half its main section to a founder card — photograph, name, job title and a personal philosophy quote. Removing only the photo would have left a name‑and‑title block that still reads as a personality page, so the whole card went. The founder image was also deleted from the image manifest rather than merely unused, so it can’t be reintroduced by accident.

The technologies section is an editorial index of the seven markets rather than a third card grid, for the same reason.

The people question is one line at the very bottom of the page — findable, playing no part in the visual identity.

“I’d like to add a Search Stories section, ideally sitting underneath Clients rather than adding another item to the main navigation. These would be short, anonymised case studies showing the thinking behind selected searches.”

“Each Search Story could follow: The Business → The Challenge → What Made the Search Difficult → How We Mapped the Market → Search Strategy → Outcome. The objective isn’t to reveal confidential client or candidate information.”

The navigation constraint drove the structure. Four stories at six stages each is too much to bury in a section on an already‑long Clients page, but you don’t want a seventh nav item. So the stories live at /clients/search-stories — underneath Clients in the URL hierarchy as well as the IA — with a summary list on the Clients page linking to each by anchor. All four stories from your examples are written, following your six stages exactly. The writing principle was that the interesting content belongs in What Made the Search Difficult and How We Mapped the Market — those two stages are where you say something a generalist recruiter couldn’t.

On anonymity: businesses are described by technology, market and region only. No client names, no candidate details, no figures traceable to a party. Outcomes are stated qualitatively rather than with identifying metrics. See open item 5 — the specifics are plausible reconstructions and need your factual check.

SECTION 06 3b148d8

Overall Visual Development

Original client feedback

“I really like the clean direction of the site and don’t want to fundamentally change it. However […] I’d like us to push the visual treatment another level and make the site feel more like a premium international industrial search consultancy.”

“Stronger use of the charcoal/yellow Consulo palette · More breathing space between major sections · Greater visual differentiation between the seven specialist markets · Avoiding too much repetition of the same card/text layouts […] It should remain clean, technical, modern and professional.”

Correction made

Palette. The three brand colours were pasted as raw hex literals in 342 places across 25 files, which makes “stronger use of the palette” almost impossible to act on. They are now named tokens, replaced mechanically, with the compiled CSS verified to produce identical values including opacity variants. Visual output is byte‑identical — what changed is that the palette now has one source of truth.

Market differentiation. This was the biggest gap: all seven market pages opened with an identical plain charcoal header, so moving between them felt like reloading the same page. Each now opens with its own photography under a charcoal gradient plus an NN/07 index marker. I deliberately did not introduce seven accent colours — that would work against strengthening the charcoal/yellow palette.

On animation: v1 already had scroll reveals, stagger, hover lifts and a reduced‑motion fallback. I left it alone rather than adding motion for its own sake, which pushes toward the flashy result you don’t want.

SECTION 07 f509e06

Insights

Original client feedback

“I really like the positioning of this as ‘Industrial Recruitment & Market Intelligence’ rather than simply calling it a blog, so let’s retain that.”

“For launch, however, I’d prefer to have three strong articles live initially and then progressively publish the others. This gives us an ongoing stream of website and LinkedIn content following launch rather than making the Insights section look artificially populated from day one.”

Correction made

Positioning untouched — the section remains “Industrial Recruitment & Market Intelligence” on both the page and the navigation.

The question was how to hold three back. Showing them as “coming soon” tiles would defeat the purpose — the section would still look populated, just with unfinished content. So scheduled articles render nowhere on the site. Publishing one is a single flag change plus a live date.

The three going live lead with method and then prove it across your two largest markets; the gearbox piece also pairs directly with one of the new Search Stories. Dropping from six to three left the page thin at the bottom, so rather than pad it I closed with a “publishing regularly” note and a LinkedIn follow prompt — which also serves the content‑stream strategy by giving visitors a reason to subscribe to the pipeline.

OPEN ITEMS

Five things blocked on your side, not on development

  1. Client logo permissions. The section is built and waiting. Each company switches on independently — artwork into public/logos and a one‑line change per company. No development work. Instructions in public/logos/README.md.
  2. Licensed photography. The treatment slots exist across market pages, About and Clients and will take real photographs without layout changes. Electrification in particular is running on placeholder artwork rather than a photo.
  3. Hotlinked market imagery. The existing photography points at an external host we don’t control (lib/data.js:1–11). If it expires, every market image on the live site breaks. Worth moving into public/ before launch.
  4. Combustion URL change. /markets/combustion-energy is now /markets/combustion-thermal. Harmless pre‑launch, but tell me if that URL has been shared and I’ll add a redirect.
  5. Search Story facts need your check. The stories follow your four examples, but the specifics — the sixty‑businesses figure, the eleven‑month timeline, the two‑quarter ramp — are my reconstructions of plausible searches, not your records. They read as real because they are specific, which is exactly why they need verifying before launch.
